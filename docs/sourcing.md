# Backup sourcing

Internal. Written 29 September 2026 after looking at CJdropshipping and
HyperSKU as alternatives to the current supplier.

Neither site could be read directly — this container's proxy blocks both — so
everything below comes from search results and from each platform's own
published material. Verify before committing money.

---

## The short version

Supplier concentration is a real risk worth fixing. But **dropshipping is the
wrong model for this product**, and the reason is dimensional, not commercial.

HyperSKU is still useful — not as a fulfilment channel, but as a sourcing agent
to find you a second factory. The better answer is to qualify two or three
factories directly and keep one as a standby.

## Why dropshipping does not work for a 900mm drain

A 900mm channel needs a carton about 95 cm long. Using CJdropshipping's own
volumetric formula (L × W × H ÷ 6000):

| | |
|---|---|
| Carton | 95 × 12 × 7 cm |
| Volumetric weight | 1.33 kg |
| Actual weight | 2.70 kg (from Cefito's published 900mm spec) |
| Chargeable | 2.70 kg |
| **Longest side** | **95 cm** |

Weight is not the problem. **Length is.** Economy lanes — ePacket and
equivalents — generally cap the longest side around 60 cm, so this parcel can
only travel express. CJ's own logistics blog puts express at *"10–20x more than
ePacket"*.

What that does to margin at the recommended $289 introductory price:

| Scenario | Unit cost | Gross margin |
|---|---:|---:|
| Current supplier, PO of 10, consolidated freight | $62 | **76.4%** |
| Current supplier, PO of 5, consolidated freight | $77 | **70.7%** |
| Current supplier, single unit | $95 | 63.8% |
| Platform price + $35 express per unit | $130 | 50.5% |
| Platform price + $50 express per unit | $145 | 44.8% |
| Platform price + $70 express per unit | $165 | 37.2% |

Every dropship row falls below the 55% floor, and those rows assume the
platform matches the current $95 — unlikely, since a dropship platform takes a
margin on top of the factory price.

The arithmetic is simple: freight on one consolidated PO of ten is a single
charge spread across ten units. Dropshipping pays it ten times.

Two more problems, independent of cost:

- **Lead time.** HyperSKU advertises 5–9 days on its China→Australia lane. The
  site currently promises *"Ships within 2 business days"*. Dropshipping from
  China makes that promise false, and it is a promise a plumber books work
  around.
- **Quality control.** The site publishes exact specifications — 3mm wedge
  wire at 4mm aperture, a 5 × 20mm full-perimeter edge bar, fully welded body,
  formed drainage base, 304 grade. Nobody inspects a dropshipped unit before it
  reaches the customer. For a brand whose whole pitch is "one product, done
  properly", that is the wrong trade.

## What each platform is actually for

### CJdropshipping

A dropship catalogue and fulfilment network with Australian warehouses. Genuine
strengths: breadth of catalogue, Shopify integration, AU stock for fast local
delivery on items it already holds.

**Fit for this product: poor.** It is built around holding popular small goods
in-market. A 900mm made-to-spec steel channel is neither small nor popular, so
it would be a per-unit express import — the worst case in the table above.

Possible narrow use: sourcing the small accessories in the product list below,
where items are light, short, and generic enough that catalogue quality is
adequate.

### HyperSKU

Positions itself as sourcing plus private label plus fulfilment, with dedicated
China→Australia lanes and no subscription. Its published process includes
sending your design to their team, who quote it and **order samples from
several factories so you can compare quality**.

**Fit as a fulfilment channel: poor**, for the same dimensional reason.

**Fit as a sourcing agent: good, and worth pursuing.** That sample-comparison
service is exactly what qualifying a backup supplier requires, and it does not
commit you to using them for fulfilment. Use them to find the factory, then buy
from the factory in consolidated POs as you do now.

One search summary put general 304 stainless linear shower drain pricing at
roughly **$7.90–$22.93 per piece**, almost certainly USD FOB and almost
certainly for small square floor wastes or grate-only items rather than 900mm
complete welded channels. **Do not read that as evidence the current supplier is
overcharging.** It is a reason to get two comparison quotes on the exact
specification, not a conclusion.

Worth asking each: MOQ, FOB price for 900mm complete units in all three widths,
grade certification, whether they weld the body or fold and seal it, sample
cost and lead time, and whether they can supply a mill certificate for the 304.

Also worth one local quote. An Australian stainless fabricator will cost
considerably more per unit, but a domestic standby that can turn a job around in
days is insurance against a shipping delay, and it would let the site make an
Australian-made claim on part of the range — something currently unclaimable.

## Spec matching is a hard constraint

This is the part most likely to cause trouble later.

The website publishes exact figures. If a backup supplier's product differs —
2.5mm wire instead of 3mm, a folded body instead of a welded one, 201 grade
passed off as 304 — then the product page becomes false the moment that stock
ships, and the technical drawings stop matching what is in the box.

So any second supplier has to either match the published specification exactly,
or the site needs a separate variant with its own honest specification. Sample
and measure before ordering. A mill certificate for the steel grade is cheap
insurance; 201 sold as 304 is a common substitution and it rusts.

### One trap

Several of those manufacturer listings describe their heelguard grates as
**"external / internal pathway trench drain"**. The site explicitly states the
product is for internal pedestrian use only, and publishes no load rating.

Selling an outdoor or trafficable grate means load-class compliance — Buda
publishes AS 3996 classes for exactly this reason. Do not extend into outdoor
or driveway drainage on a supplier's marketing copy. That is a separate product
with separate certification, and getting it wrong is a safety issue, not a
marketing one.

## Suggested sequence

1. Ask the current supplier the two open questions from `docs/pricing.md`:
   is $95 landed or ex-works, and is GST payable on import on top.
2. Send the exact specification to HyperSKU's sourcing team and to two of the
   factories above. Ask for MOQ, FOB, sample cost and lead time.
3. Order one sample from each. Measure the wire, the aperture and the edge bar
   against the published figures. Request a mill certificate for the steel.
4. Get one Australian fabricator quote as a domestic standby.
5. Keep the current supplier as primary while a second is qualified. The point
   of a backup is that it is already tested when it is needed.

Cost scenarios can be modelled in the pricing desk — the landed cost field
accepts any figure, so a candidate supplier's quote plus freight can be tested
against the ladder before committing.
